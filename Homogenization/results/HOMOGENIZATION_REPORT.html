This report documents an end-to-end homogenization of the pre-1958 Malaysia/Singapore monthly rainfall archive: how artificial breaks (inhomogeneities) were detected against a network of neighbouring stations, how the affected series were corrected, how gaps were filled, and how an independent test was used to grade the confidence of each break. The dominant artificial signal is a cluster of breaks in the years immediately after the Second World War.

Tooling note. The genuine climatol and RHtestsV4 R packages could not be installed in the execution sandbox (CRAN, conda and r-universe are firewalled there). Results were produced by a faithful Python implementation of the published algorithms, validated against synthetic series with known injected breaks. Ready-to-run scripts for the real R packages (run_climatol.R, run_rhtests.R) are included so the analysis reproduces on a CRAN-connected machine; small numerical differences from the packaged tools are expected but the break structure should be stable.

1. Data and scope

The source archive merges historical monthly rainfall (in inches) for the Malay Peninsula and Singapore with data-rescue records from Sumatra, Borneo and the intervening islands. Records were restricted to year ≤ 1958 — the year the peninsular series close — giving 14,549 station-years from 672 stations. This restricted core is denser and more complete than the full archive (monthly missing 6.5%, versus 10% with the post-1958 tail included). Seven previously-empty Selangor stations (Bute Estate Nilai, Tandridge, Tanjong Karang D.I.D., Pulau Lumut, Sungei Gumut, Sungei Kalong, Ulu Langat) were populated from newly transcribed records before this run and are now part of the network.

The defining feature for homogenization is that these are many short, staggered, overlapping records: a median of ~18–26 data-years per station, with only 164 reaching 30 years. Combined with the low spatial correlation of equatorial convective rainfall and the absence of any station-move metadata, this makes every detected break undocumented and places the archive among the harder homogenization problems.

2. Method

2.1 Preparation and quality control

The wide station-year-by-month table was reshaped onto a common 1875–1958 monthly frame (1,008 months × 672 stations with coordinates), plus annual totals (formed only where all 12 months are present) and monsoon-season totals. Detection is performed on annual totals, which are more Gaussian and have higher signal-to-noise for rainfall; corrections are applied at the monthly level. Per the agreed QC rules, physically-impossible values (negative or >150 in/month) were masked — none occurred, so all real extremes were kept — and reconcile-flagged rows were retained. Stations with ≥10 data-years were treated as eligible for homogenization (529 stations); shorter ones are carried through for infilling only.

2.2 Neighbour network

Because rainfall de-correlates quickly in space here, each station's reference is a distance-weighted mean of its normalized neighbours, capped at the 10 nearest within 200 km (Gaussian weighting, 60 km scale) and requiring a minimum annual-anomaly correlation of 0.10. 525 eligible stations obtained at least one usable neighbour; the very thin regions (Singapore, Perlis, Province Wellesley, Sabah, Sarawak) are effectively absolute-only and are flagged accordingly.

2.3 Detection — Climatol method (SNHT on a relative series)

Each series was ratio-normalized (divided by its mean — the precipitation-appropriate normalization). A neighbour reference was built in ratio space and the candidate-minus-reference anomaly series was tested with the Standard Normal Homogeneity Test, applied recursively to find multiple breakpoints (minimum segment 5 years, 95% Monte-Carlo critical values). References were then rebuilt from the provisionally-corrected series and detection repeated — two outer passes — so that a single broken neighbour cannot contaminate the references.

2.4 Correction and infilling

Corrections are multiplicative: each earlier segment is scaled onto the level of the most-recent (reference) segment, using the ratio of candidate to reference over each segment. Missing months were filled from a seasonal (monthly-climatology) neighbour reference, denormalized to the station's homogenized level, and only within each station's observed span — gap-filling, never extrapolation beyond a station's life. Two products are produced: an NA-preserving series (observed values corrected, gaps left as NA) and an infilled series.

2.5 Independent confirmation — RHtests style

On the 164 stations with ≥30 data-years, two penalized maximal tests were run: the PMF (penalized maximal F, a two-phase regression with a common trend and no reference) directly on the annual totals, and the PMT (penalized maximal t, on the candidate-minus-reference series). Both use Monte-Carlo critical values, which supply the end-point penalty and multiplicity correction directly.

2.6 Validation of the implementation

Before running on real data, the detection code was validated on synthetic networks: 7 of 8 injected relative breaks were recovered within ±3 years, a base-series step was located exactly, and the SNHT critical values reproduced the standard tables (n=20→7.0, n=40→7.9, n=60→9.1). Correctness of the correction was checked on the real output: unadjusted stations are bit-identical to the raw data, no negative values are introduced, and extremes are preserved.

3. Results

3.1 Detection overview

The Climatol method detected 78 breaks across 70 stations. The median multiplicative correction was 0.89 (a typical ~11% reduction of inflated early segments; applied factors range 0.69–1.91), and 19,813 interior monthly gaps were filled within span.

3.2 Temporal structure — the post-war discontinuity

Breaks are not uniformly distributed in time: 41% of all detected breaks fall in 1946–1953, peaking in 1948–1950. This is the archive's dominant artificial signal — network disruption, station relocation and re-establishment around the Japanese occupation and its aftermath — and any trend analysis spanning the war must account for it.

3.3 Cross-method agreement and confidence

Of the 49 Climatol breaks on long stations, 17 (35%) were confirmed by RHtests within ±2 years (16 by PMT, 6 by PMF). The modest rate is expected, not alarming: PMF is an absolute test with weak power for artificial breaks in noisy tropical series, and PMT's trend-aware penalty is deliberately stricter than SNHT — so RHtests endorses the larger, clearer breaks and leaves marginal ones as Climatol-only. Every break is therefore graded by confidence rather than treated as binary: 16 high, 1 medium-high, 32 medium, 29 low, plus 55 RHtests-only breaks recorded for information.

3.5 Spatial pattern

Detected breaks concentrate in the dense western-peninsula regions (Perak, Selangor, Johor, Pahang), simply because those are the only places with enough neighbours to run a reliable relative test. The east coast, Borneo and the islands contribute few breaks — not because they are homogeneous, but because they are too sparse to test.

3.6 Example corrections

The six longest adjusted stations — all in Perak and Pahang — illustrate the mechanism. Each has a single break whose pre-break segment reads systematically high relative to neighbours and is scaled down; the raw and homogenized lines coincide after the break and diverge before it. Note the 1918 break recurring at two Perak hospitals, a plausible shared administrative/network change worth checking against station history.

3.7 The mm → inches merge boundary

The Sumatra/Borneo records were transcribed in millimetres and converted to inches on ingestion, making their provenance a candidate offset. Pre-1958, only a handful of island stations are long and well-connected enough to test, and just 3 island-sourced breaks were found — too few to assess the conversion boundary statistically. It should be spot-checked manually rather than relied upon here.

5. Caveats and recommendations

Detection is more reliable than correction for this archive: correlations are low, series are short and staggered, and all breaks are undocumented. Corrections were applied for every Climatol break (the primary method); for a conservative product, restrict adjustments to confidence ∈ {high, medium-high} using the inventory. Thin-region and short-station breaks (confidence = low) rest on few neighbours and should be treated as tentative. The infilled product is spatial interpolation within a station's active period, not observed data — use the NA-preserving copy for anything sensitive to that distinction. Finally, the post-war break cluster and the recurring 1918 Perak break are the two features most worth checking against any surviving station histories.
